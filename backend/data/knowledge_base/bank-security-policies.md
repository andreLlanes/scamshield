---
title: Bank and Card Issuer Security Policies
category: banking
authority: industry-standard
---

# Banks never ask for OTPs, PINs, or passwords

Legitimate banks, card issuers, and e-wallet providers never ask a customer to
reveal a one-time password (OTP), a PIN, an online banking password, or the
three-digit CVV printed on the back of a card. This holds on inbound calls,
outbound calls, SMS, email, and chat. Any caller who asks for these — whatever
department they claim to be from — is attempting credential theft. A genuine
fraud team can already see and act on an account without the customer reciting
a code.

An OTP exists precisely to prove that the person holding the phone authorised a
specific transaction. Reading it aloud transfers that authorisation to the
caller. Banks print this warning in the OTP message itself.

# Banks do not move money to a "safe account"

There is no such thing as a "safe account", "holding account", or "temporary
protection account" that a bank asks a customer to transfer money into. If a
bank believes an account is compromised, it freezes or blocks the account from
its own systems. It does not ask the customer to move funds anywhere. A request
to transfer a balance for safekeeping is a scam in every case.

# Banks let you call back and take your time

A real bank representative will accept being called back on the number printed
on the back of the card or on the official website. They will not object to a
customer hanging up to verify. Refusing a callback, insisting the customer stay
on the line, or claiming the line must not be disconnected is a manipulation
tactic, not a security procedure.

# Card blocking and reversal do not require caller-provided codes

To block a card or dispute a transaction, a bank needs to authenticate the
customer through its own channels — app confirmation, branch visit, or callback
to a registered number. It does not require the customer to read out a code
received by SMS. Disputes are filed with a reference number issued by the bank
to the customer, not the other way around.

# Account closure warnings are not delivered by phone deadline

Banks do not permanently close or lock accounts within minutes of a phone call.
Regulatory notice periods, written notification, and documented processes apply.
A caller imposing a countdown ("your account will be locked in ten minutes") is
manufacturing urgency.

# Remote access tools are never part of bank support

No bank asks a customer to install AnyDesk, TeamViewer, QuickSupport, or any
screen-sharing tool so staff can "secure" a device or "complete a reversal".
Screen sharing lets the caller watch the customer type credentials and lets them
operate the banking app directly.
