---
title: How Government Agencies Actually Contact People
category: government
authority: procedural
---

# Arrest warrants are not settled over the phone

Law enforcement agencies do not call people to announce a warrant of arrest and
then offer to cancel it in exchange for payment. Warrants are issued by courts
and served in person. Any caller offering to "settle", "clear", or "lift" a
warrant over the phone in exchange for a fee is running an impersonation scam.
Paying a fee to avoid arrest is not a procedure that exists.

# Agencies do not demand payment by e-wallet, gift card, or crypto

Tax authorities, customs, police, and social security agencies collect money
through official channels: accredited banks, government payment portals,
authorised payment centres, and official receipts. They never request payment
via GCash to a personal number, gift cards, Google Play or Steam codes,
cryptocurrency, or a wire transfer to an individual. Demanding these payment
methods is one of the clearest single indicators of a scam.

# Tax and benefit matters arrive in writing first

Assessments, deficiency notices, and benefit suspensions are communicated
through written notice to a registered address, with a case or assessment
number the recipient can verify independently. A first contact by phone
demanding immediate payment, with no prior written notice, does not match how
these agencies operate.

# Social security and identification numbers are not "suspended"

A national ID, TIN, or social security number is not suspended, blocked, or
deactivated because of "suspicious activity". This claim has no basis in any
agency's process; it exists only in scam scripts.

# Customs does not phone recipients about held parcels

Where duties are genuinely owed on an imported parcel, the courier or the
customs office issues a written assessment with a tracking reference, payable
through official channels. A phone call demanding an immediate "release fee" or
"clearance fee" to a personal account, especially paired with a threat about
illegal contents, is a parcel scam.

# Real investigators do not demand secrecy from the public

Callers claiming to run a confidential investigation and instructing a person
not to tell family, bank staff, or police are isolating the victim. Genuine
investigations do not require a member of the public to conceal the contact,
and no agency instructs a person to hide a payment from their own bank.
