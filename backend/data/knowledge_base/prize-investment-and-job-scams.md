---
title: Prize, Investment, Loan and Job Offer Scams
category: offers
authority: industry-standard
---

# You cannot win a raffle you never entered

Legitimate promotions draw winners from registered entrants and can show the
entry record. A call announcing a win in a raffle the recipient never joined is
a prize scam. Registered promotions also publish winners through official
channels and permit claiming in person without prepayment.

# Legitimate prizes never require the winner to pay first

Where taxes apply to a prize, they are withheld from the prize or settled on
release at an official venue with documentation. No genuine promotion asks a
winner to send a processing, release, or tax fee in advance, and none accepts
gift-card codes.

# Guaranteed returns do not exist

No investment can guarantee a fixed high return with no risk. Claims of
guaranteed weekly or monthly percentages, "no risk" capital, or doubling money
within a set period describe a Ponzi structure, not an investment product.
Legitimate investment products are sold by licensed entities, come with written
risk disclosure, and give the investor time to read it.

# Pressure to decide immediately is incompatible with regulated selling

Licensed financial products come with a cooling-off period and written
documentation. "Slots close today", "this rate is only for this call", and
"do not tell anyone or the slot goes to someone else" are pressure and isolation
tactics designed to prevent the target from checking the offer.

# Approved loans do not require an advance payment

Lenders deduct fees and insurance from the released amount or bill them after
disbursement. A lender that requires an up-front transfer before releasing funds
is running an advance-fee scam. A genuine pre-approved offer can also be
verified by calling the lender's published number.

# Employers do not charge applicants

Legitimate employers and licensed recruitment agencies do not charge candidates
for training materials, uniforms, IDs, processing, or placement. Job offers made
without any interview, with unusually high pay for minimal work, and with an
up-front payment requirement are recruitment scams.

# Family emergency claims should be verified on a known number

A caller claiming a relative is hurt, arrested, or detained, and demanding money
immediately while insisting the family not be told, is running an emergency
scam. The claim is checkable in minutes by hanging up and calling the relative
directly on their usual number.
