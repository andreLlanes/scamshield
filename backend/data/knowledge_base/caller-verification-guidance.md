title: Verifying an Unknown Caller
category: guidance
authority: guidance

# Caller ID proves nothing

Caller ID and SMS sender names are trivially spoofed. A call that appears to
come from a bank's hotline, a government office, or a saved contact can be
placed from anywhere. The displayed number must never be treated as
authentication, and a matching sender name in an SMS thread does not confirm the
message is genuine.

# Hang up and dial the number you already have

The single most reliable check is to end the call and dial the number printed on
the back of the card, on an official statement, or on the organisation's
published website. Do not use a number the caller supplies, and do not use a
number found through a search-engine advertisement. Wait a moment or use another
handset so the original call has genuinely disconnected.

# Real organisations tolerate verification

No legitimate caller is harmed by the recipient hanging up to verify. Objections
to verification — "there is no time", "the line must stay open", "the case will
close" — are themselves the warning sign.

# Knowing your details does not prove identity

Callers frequently open with a name, address, birthday, partial card number, or
recent purchase. That information is widely available through data breaches and
public records. Possessing personal details is not evidence that the caller
works for the institution they claim.

# Report and preserve

If a call turns out to be a scam, report it to the institution being
impersonated and to the national cybercrime or consumer protection channel.
Keeping the recording, the number, and the timestamps materially helps
investigation. If money was already sent, contacting the bank immediately is the
only realistic chance of a recall.

# What to do while still on the call

Do not confirm personal details, do not read out any code, do not install any
application, and do not agree to a transfer. Saying "I will call you back on my
bank's published number" and hanging up is always a safe response, and costs
nothing if the call happens to be genuine.
