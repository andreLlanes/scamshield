---
title: Payment Requests That Indicate Fraud
category: payments
authority: industry-standard
---

# Gift card and load codes are never a legitimate payment method

No bank, government agency, utility, courier, employer, or law-enforcement body
accepts payment in Google Play, Steam, Apple, or prepaid load codes. Once a code
is read aloud, the value is gone and cannot be reversed or traced. A request for
a gift-card code is conclusive evidence of fraud regardless of what the caller
claims the payment is for.

# Advance fees to release money are a fee scam

Genuine prizes, refunds, loan releases, inheritances, and salary payments are
never conditioned on the recipient paying a fee first. "Processing fee",
"release fee", "clearance fee", "insurance fee", "tax fee", and "registration
fee" are the standard vocabulary of advance-fee fraud. Legitimate deductions are
netted from the amount, not collected in advance from the recipient.

# Payments to personal accounts are a mismatch signal

Institutions collect into corporate accounts in the institution's registered
name. A request to send money to an individual's e-wallet number or personal
bank account, while claiming to represent a company or agency, is a mismatch
between the claimed identity and the payment destination.

# Irreversible rails are chosen deliberately

Cryptocurrency transfers, remittance-centre cash pickups, and e-wallet transfers
to strangers cannot be recalled once collected. Scammers prefer them for exactly
that reason. A caller steering away from reversible methods — card payment,
bank-to-bank transfer with a named beneficiary, payment through an official
portal — is choosing irreversibility on purpose.

# Refund overpayment scripts are reversed theft

A caller claiming an accidental refund or overpayment, then asking for the
"excess" back by transfer or gift card, is running a refund scam. The original
credit is typically fake, reversed, or made with stolen funds; the money sent
back is real.

# Urgency around payment is the tell

Legitimate payment obligations survive a day's delay for verification. A caller
who insists the transfer must happen during the call, before the customer can
consult a family member, visit a branch, or call the institution back, is
optimising for the victim not having time to check.
